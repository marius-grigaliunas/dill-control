# 0052 Five core weapons with two tiers each, specials, six tiers a weapon, and a shop on the ship

*22-23 October 2026. Decided, not built. Replaces the pitch's arsenal and the
plan's proposed slice arsenal; supersedes the random kill-based upgrades of the
pitch. Builds on 0050 and
0051.*

## Context

The prototype arsenal was about nine weapons tuned piecemeal; the plan proposed
a machete, Brine Ripper, shotgun, rocket launcher and grenade. Progression was a
random company package every so many kills, with no pick screen. Each weapon
needs one distinct job, or players settle on the strongest five.

## Decision

**Five core weapons**, each with two tiers, in fixed slots in the slice. The
grapple sits on top of all five (middle mouse button); the mouse wheel switches
weapons and the right mouse button aims on every one.

| Weapon | Job | Tier 1 | Tier 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Assault rifle | The heavy, punchy workhorse | Piercing rounds | Underbarrel grenade launcher, lobbed in an arc |
| Bazooka | A straight rocket from a single tube | Four-rocket volley | Bigger, stronger rockets |
| Pole saw | Company-issue melee: a long pole with a chainsaw blade, swung like a polearm; fun, powerful and satisfying **with reach**, never close-quarters fiddling. **No ammo, always available** | Longer reach | Spin attack |
| Laser cannon | A sweeping destructive beam like the bike's, deliberately imprecise, about 5 s cooldown; the main vine-cutting tool. Also a gravity-gun mode (Half-Life 2 style) that pulls and punts cucumbers and debris, not upgraded in the slice | Shorter cooldown | A literally wider beam |
| Sniper | A very high calibre .50 long rifle: "the air belongs to you" | Explosive rounds | Airstrike |

Later upgrades turn the pole saw into a ranged weapon too. These replace the
prototype arsenal and the plan's machete, Brine Ripper, shotgun, rocket launcher
and grenade; the rest waits in the lab.

**Specials** are of two kinds. *Reusable* specials are found, kept, refilled and
carried into any contract (a fire rocket launcher, the minigun). *Consumable*
super weapons are found with a few charges and gone when spent (the nuke
designator, an orbital laser, a hand atomic grenade, tomahawk or javelin
missiles). The nuke designator marks a spot and you must fly clear before it
lands, so it uses movement.

**Slots:** five, with free choice between core weapons and reusable specials. A
separate **consumables bar** holds grenades, medkits, shield generators, ammo and
found super weapons.

**Six tiers a weapon:** ammo, ammo, mod, mod, secondary ability, and that
ability's evolution. Mods specialise. Tiers persist across the campaign.

**The shop.** Upgrades are bought in a shop in the ship's cabin with invoice
money (0051's two-purchase limit applies in the slice). Hovering shows the
details of everything, and the money only stretches so far. This replaces the
random kill-based packages with no pick screen.

**Company drops inside a contract** stay: physical pickups with loaned support,
lasting the contract and never touching persistent progression (an earlier drop proposal). They are
fighter and tank mechs, and at the top tier the laser hoverbike.

**The hoverbike is a special item.** It is a fast scout, used to get from A to B. Its gun, rocket pod, bomb bay and laser are kept, but every one has limited ammo, which limits the bike's power; the laser fires for a spell, then cools (10 s in contract 2). It is the prize
of contract 2 and a company drop at 20,000 kills in contract 3 (0051), not a
rental and not the way a contract begins.

## Costs and open ends

- **Five slots with free choice** risk players settling on the strongest five;
  each weapon's distinct job is the defence, and only play tests it.
- What unlocks persistent tiers in the full game is open, with special-weapon
  charge counts and the kill thresholds of later drops.
- The bike's ammo counts and the laser's cooldown are numbers for its stats resource, not yet set.
- Rocket balance is parked: one bazooka load killing 7,000-8,000 may be a lack
  of pressure on the player rather than a kill-count problem. Rockets stay
  plentiful, because they are the fun.
- Detailed ballistics and wound channels, and magic weapons, are backlog.
